My investments

About this

Everything on this tab comes from the game: what your characters hold and your auction house purchases and sales, read from the WoW Forever Companion addon's file on your PC (see Info, Inputs). The Show buttons switch between Total, only the items you bought (Invested) and only the items you collected (Collected). Collected items cost nothing, so they count fully as profit. If you hold an item that you both bought and collected, the units you still hold are split in the same proportion as you acquired them. The gold tiles, Hold or sell and Profit history always show everything.

Net Worth is what you hold at each item's cheapest current price. Profit is what your sales have made, so it stays 0 until you sell; under it, if sold today is what you would make if you also sold everything you hold at today's lowest prices after the 5% auction cut, minus what it cost. Press the Net Worth or Profit tile to pick which columns the positions table under the tiles shows.

The second row of tiles: Gold now is the gold on all your characters, Gold + items adds what your items are worth, and Goal progress compares that with your goal. All three come from the game file; press one to see the inputs.

Share of portfolio

About this

How your portfolio is split between items. Money invested shows how your spending is divided, so a big slice means a lot of your gold is tied up in that item. Number of items shows how many units of each you hold. Money by category groups the same spending by item class, or by item type when you have picked a class. The seven biggest slices get a name; everything smaller is added up in the grey Other slice (hover it to see what is in it). Number of items counts single units, not stacks, so something cheap you hold a lot of, like arrows, can be one of the seven. Press a slice or name to open the item.

Profit if sold today, per item

About this

For each item you hold: what you would receive selling all your units at today's lowest price, after the 5% auction cut, minus what you paid for them. Profit from earlier sales is not in these bars (it is in the Profit column of the table above). Green bars are gains, red bars are losses. It is a paper result until you sell. The chart starts grouped by item class: press a bar to open a class, then a type, then see its items. The buttons above the chart take you back.

Value over time

About this

What your holdings were worth at each snapshot. Pick Total, a whole category or type, or any items below to draw them together; the list covers everything you hold. The time buttons choose the period: the last 24 hours (one point per hour), the last week (one point per day) or all time (one point per day, month or year depending on how much data there is). Each dotted line is what you have invested in that line's items, in the same colour. Hover the chart to see the values, how many units each line covers and the price per unit (each, for a single item; the average per unit for Total and categories). Worth also moves when you buy or sell units, because it is units held times price. To compare one item's price per unit with your avg buy price, press it to open it in the Explorer: its price chart shows your avg buy price as a dashed line.

Profit history
About this

Realised profit from the sales you recorded, adding up over time. Each step is a day with sales. The profit of a sale is what you received minus the avg cost per unit (collected = free). The bars and the Sales per item table show the profit or loss per item sold. It stays empty until you sell something on the auction house; sales arrive by themselves from the auction house mails the addon reads.

Sales per item

About this

Profit from what you sold, per item. Avg sale price is what you received per unit after the cut, Received is the total, and Profit / loss compares it with the avg cost per unit (collected = free) of the units sold. Sales are recorded from the game by themselves.

About this

An estimate of how many units sold between two snapshots, from auctions that disappeared and were not about to expire. It misses items listed and sold between snapshots and counts cancellations as sales, so treat it as a rough guide.

Cheapest price vs next price level
About this

The cheapest listing price and the next price level above it, saved about every 4 hours. When the two lines are far apart, buying the cheapest units and reselling just below the next level pays. If the gap keeps closing quickly, other flippers are finding it too.

About this

Average price by hour of the day compared with the item's overall average. Green hours are cheaper than average, so a better time to buy. Red hours are pricier, a better time to sell. It only means something after about two days of data.

Flip scanner
About this

A flip is buying a few units that are listed well below the price most sellers ask, and reselling them at that price. Most units at is the price that half of all units for sale are at or below (the bulk of the market). Only units listed at 80% of that or less count as cheap, and only when they are at most 30% of all units, so an item where nearly everything sits at the low price is never a flip. The cheap units must also cost at most 90% of the item's normal price (its average lowest price over 7 days): if the lowest price is usually that low, it is simply the real price. Buy at is the average price of those cheap units, Resell at is the first price above them, and Return and Profit are after the 5% cut. The item must also sell: at least 1 a day, and the cheap units must sell within about a week. Prices come from your own AH scan when it is newer than the hourly data, so scan in game for flips that are still there.

Use the filters to hide items with few listings, items that sell slowly, and grey junk. Rarity and fewest listings also apply to the Opportunity map.

From the game
About this

The WoW Forever Companion addon saves what every character carries in bags, bank, mail, auctions and equipped, their gold and profession levels, and your auction house purchases and sales (read from the auction house mails). The game writes it to your PC when you log out or type /reload, and this page reads that file every time you open it or come back to the tab, so there is nothing to import. Places a character has not visited yet (bank, mailbox, auction house) fill in once they do. The dashboard counts everything you own across all characters in your Portfolio: items that are not in your purchase log count as collected (cost nothing). Purchases and sales go into the purchase log by themselves, sales at what you received after the 5% cut, and never twice. Guild banks are never included; a warband bank shows as its own line.

    Items without a name

    About this

    The auction house feed only contains item IDs. Names come from Blizzard's item data, which does not know every item yet (mostly WoW Forever-only items). Such items are missing from search and from the scanners until Blizzard adds them. The collector retries them on every run. Item ID is the number you see in a database or on the item's page. Listings is how many auctions the item has now.

    Item IDLowest nowListingsUnits

    Where the numbers come from

    A collector on GitHub reads Blizzard's auction house data for the Horde house of the EU PvP ruleset every hour and saves a snapshot whenever the prices have changed. This page is rebuilt by itself after every new snapshot. Your own holdings, gold and trades are read from the addon's file on your PC and never leave it.

    The words used on the page are explained under Glossary.

    Estimated sales

    Each snapshot, the collector compares the auctions with the previous one. An auction that disappeared counts as expired if it was in the shortest time bucket before, and as sold otherwise. Cancelled auctions look like sales, and items listed and sold between two snapshots are invisible, so treat sold per day as a rough lower-quality estimate.

    The rules behind the suggestions

    Buy watch: at least 8% below the average price and at least 10 listings. Consider selling: at least 10% above the average price and above your average buy price. Hold, don't add yet: at least 10% below normal and still falling over 24 hours. Could add more: at least 8% below normal and not falling. These are simple signals, not predictions.

    Limits

    Items with very few listings are not tracked here. Valuing at the lowest buyout is optimistic for large stacks. The auction house keeps a 5% cut of every sale.

    Glossary

    Your price terms

    The same names are used everywhere in the dashboard. Per unit means for one item.

    Prices (g, s, c)
    Amounts are shown as g (gold), s (silver) and c (copper): 100c = 1s and 100s = 1g. Hover an amount to see it written out.
    Lowest now (lowest buyout)
    The cheapest single-unit price listed right now. It is what you would pay to buy one, and what the page uses to value what you hold.
    Median now
    The quantity-weighted middle price: half of the listed units cost less, half cost more. It shows the typical price when the cheapest listing is an odd one.
    Vendor value
    What an NPC vendor pays per unit. It is a guaranteed floor you can always sell at.
    Your avg buy price
    What you paid per unit on average for the units you bought. Units you collected for free are not counted in it.
    Paid total
    What you paid in total for the units you still hold.
    After 5% cut (total)
    What all your units would bring in if sold at the lowest price now, after the auction house keeps 5%.
    Net Worth (Worth)
    Your units times the lowest price, before the 5% cut. It is higher than what you would really receive. Gold + items on Portfolio adds your gold to it.
    Break-even sell price
    The lowest price you must sell at to get back what you paid after the 5% cut: your price divided by 0.95.

    Other terms

    Listing
    One auction on the house: a seller offering a stack at a price. Ten listings of the same item means ten sellers or stacks.
    Units for sale
    The total number of items across all listings of that item. One listing can hold many units.
    Most units at
    The price that half of all units for sale are at or below: where the bulk of the market sits. The flip scanner resells near it.
    Price ladder
    The cheapest few price levels of an item with how many units sit at each. It shows how much a big purchase pushes the price up.
    Normal price (average) / vs normal
    The mean lowest buyout over the snapshots from the last 7 days (shorter while data builds up; it needs at least 4 snapshots spanning 12 hours). "vs normal" is how far today's price is from it.
    24 h
    The change since the snapshot about 24 hours earlier.
    Sold / day
    An estimate of units sold per day, taken from auctions that disappeared between snapshots without having been about to expire. Rough.
    Days of supply
    Units for sale divided by units sold per day: how long today's stock would take to sell.
    Expired share
    The share of vanished auctions that expired instead of selling. High means sellers struggle to sell.
    Flip
    Buying a few units listed well below where most units sit and reselling them at that price, after the auction house cut.
    Auction cut
    The fee the house keeps when something sells. It is 5% of the sale price.
    Avg cost per unit (collected = free)
    Everything you paid for an item divided by all the units you got, bought and collected. Profit from sales is worked out with it. It is not your avg buy price, which leaves collected units out.
    Profit (profit from sales)
    Realised profit: what you received minus the avg cost per unit (collected = free) of the units sold. It is 0 until you sell something.
    Profit if sold today
    Profit from sales plus what you would make selling everything you hold at today's lowest prices, after the 5% cut, minus what it cost you.
    Margin
    Profit per craft as a share of the reagent cost, after the auction cut on the product.
    Writ
    Craftsman's Writ: an item traded on the auction house and linked to a craftable item. It is tracked next to that item.
